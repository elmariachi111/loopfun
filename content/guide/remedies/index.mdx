## Why this section exists

The [shortcomings](/guide/shortcomings) section maps what goes wrong with loops. The [idea board](/guide/field-notes/idea-board) lists research directions. This section asks a narrower, more practical question:

> Which of these problems could a **small, general-purpose service** take off a loop builder's plate, without knowing anything about what the loop is for?

"General-purpose" is the constraint that matters. A sales loop, a coding loop and an inbox-triage loop have nothing in common at the level of their goals. They have a lot in common at the level of their **plumbing**: they all call models, call tools with side effects, retry, wait, spend money and finish (or don't). That plumbing is what these proposals target.

## The problems, restated

| What we keep seeing | Where the guide documents it | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Every loop rebuilds credentials, retries, rate limits, budgets and state storage for each external tool it touches. A re-run iteration can repeat a side effect (a second email, a second call). | [Reliability](/guide/shortcomings/reliability), [Security](/guide/shortcomings/security) | [A control plane for loop side effects](/guide/remedies/control-plane) |
| Loops pay interactive prices for work that could wait hours, and polling while waiting costs more still. | [Cost](/guide/shortcomings/cost), [Pacing](/guide/goals/budgets-and-lifecycle#pacing-what-a-goal-costs-while-it-waits) | [Off-peak tokens and arbitrage](/guide/remedies/off-peak-tokens) |
| Nobody can say what a goal cost, whether it was really achieved, or which iterations were wasted. Loop state lives where nobody can inspect it. | [Reliability and observability](/guide/shortcomings/reliability#observability-is-thin), [Verification](/guide/shortcomings/verification) | [Loop-native reporting](/guide/remedies/loop-reporting) |

## How they fit together

The three ideas share one premise: **if every model call and every tool call a loop makes passes through one place, and each call is tagged with the goal and iteration it belongs to**, then the other features become cheap:

<Flow
  steps={['Loop iteration', 'One egress (tagged by goal + iteration)', 'Models and tools', 'Event log', 'Per-goal reports']}
  caption="Routing to a cheaper tier is a policy at the egress. Per-goal cost and inefficiency detection are queries over its event log."
/>

So the sensible order is probably:

1. **Tag and log.** A thin proxy that changes nothing about behavior, only attaches goal, run and iteration ids and records what happened. This alone answers "what did that goal cost?"
2. **Report.** Inefficiency detectors and achievement triage run over that log. This is the part loop builders can't easily get anywhere else.
3. **Control.** Budgets, circuit breakers and idempotency for side effects, once the log shows where the money and the damage actually go.
4. **Route.** Deadline-aware routing to flex, batch and off-peak tiers, with savings reported per goal.

## What we're deliberately not proposing

- **Another harness or agent framework.** Every vendor already ships one. These services should sit *beside* Claude Code, Codex, Hermes or a hand-rolled loop, not replace them.
- **Use-case agents.** No "sales loop" or "support loop". Use-case-neutral or nothing.
- **Reselling anyone's subscription quota.** See the note on [idle capacity](/guide/remedies/off-peak-tokens#two-meanings-of-idle-capacity).
