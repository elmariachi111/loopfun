## The problem

Loops pay **interactive prices for non-interactive work**. A lot of what a loop does has no human waiting on the other end:

- overnight goal runs and backlog burn-downs
- judges and evaluators checked at the end of an iteration
- compaction and summarization of long histories
- [ambient](/guide/patterns/ambient-loops) heartbeats and scheduled sweeps
- evals, backfills and re-processing jobs

All of it typically runs on the same standard tier as a chat reply. Meanwhile providers have spare capacity at night and between traffic peaks, and are increasingly willing to sell it cheaper. The question is how a loop can buy it without anyone hand-tuning each call.

## Who sells cheaper tokens today

The short answer: **nobody major sells a true, market-clearing token spot price.** What exists in November 2026 is a set of flat discounts in exchange for latency or pre-emption, plus some early open marketplaces. The real spot markets sit one layer down, at GPU rental.

| Mechanism | Who | Typical discount | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Batch APIs** | OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon Bedrock, Mistral | about 50% | Asynchronous; results within up to 24 hours ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/message-batches-api), [Gemini](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/optimization)) <Evidence level="primary" /> |
| **Flex tiers** (synchronous, pre-emptible) | OpenAI `service_tier: "flex"` ([docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/flex-processing.md)), Gemini Flex, preview ([docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/flex-inference)), Bedrock Flex ([docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/service-tiers-inference.md)) | about 50% | Slower; may be rejected (OpenAI returns a 429 at no charge) or evicted when standard traffic spikes. Gemini doesn't fall back to standard on its own, and Flex traffic still counts toward rate limits. <Evidence level="primary" /> |
| **Priority tiers** (the inverse) | Bedrock Priority | a premium, reportedly about 75% ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/amazon-bedrock-pricing-tiers-flex-priority-batch-2026/)) | You pay more to jump the queue. Useful for the few loop steps that truly block a human. <Evidence level="secondary" /> |
| **Time-of-day pricing** | DeepSeek, peak and off-peak billing since mid-August 2026 | off-peak about half of peak | Fixed UTC windows; published figures disagree across sources ([Synthorai](https://synthorai.io/blog/llm-api-price-changes-2026/)). <Evidence level="secondary" /> |
| **Aggregator routing** | OpenRouter `:floor` variant | cheapest provider for the model | Sorts providers by price **and** makes flex-tier endpoints eligible; `provider.sort: "price"` does price sorting without flex ([docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/model-variants/floor)). <Evidence level="primary" /> |
| **Open inference marketplaces** | Surplus Intelligence (order book for spare capacity, launched May 2026, settles in USDC on Base), Chutes (Bittensor subnet 64) | claimed 40 to 60% | Self-reported savings; crypto settlement; harder questions about who serves your data ([Alea Research](https://alearesearch.substack.com/p/surplus-intelligence-an-order-book), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/chutes-surplus-onchain-inference-growth/)). <Evidence level="anecdotal" /> |
| **GPU spot and futures** (below the token layer) | SF Compute (hourly H100/H200 market), AWS and GCP spot VMs (up to 90%+ off), compute futures from CME and Silicon Data | large, but variable | You run the model yourself, and capacity can be reclaimed at any time ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/sf-compute-0739be6d/)). <Evidence level="secondary" /> |

Two notes on OpenRouter, since it's the closest thing to a ready-made pass-through:

- `:floor` already does the "use the flex tier when it happens to be cheapest" part. What it doesn't know is **whether your request can afford to wait**.
- Third-party posts report that on **October 28, 2026** OpenRouter changed its default routing to optimize a "Routing Cost" (price plus waiting plus retries) rather than raw price, with requests about 6% more expensive and far fewer 429s ([AIREITER](https://aireiter.com/blog/openrouter-provider-routing-explained)). We couldn't confirm this in OpenRouter's own docs. <Evidence level="anecdotal" /> If true, it's the market moving toward exactly the cost-versus-latency trade-off this page is about.

## Why loops are the ideal flex customer

Flex and off-peak tiers suit loops better than they suit almost anything else:

- **Loops already wait.** A goal waiting on CI for ten minutes doesn't notice a one-to-fifteen-minute flex latency target.
- **Loops already retry.** A flex 429 is one more retry, which the loop (or [its control plane](/guide/remedies/control-plane)) handles anyway.
- **Loops already have budgets.** "Finish by 7am for under $20" is a natural way to state a goal, and a natural input for a scheduler.

<Callout type="warn" title="The catch: prompt caching">
  Loop economics are dominated by **cache hits**: every iteration re-sends a long, mostly stable prefix (see
  [Cost](/guide/shortcomings/cost)). Moving a running conversation to a different provider, or sometimes a different
  tier, can throw that cache away. A 50% discount on a request that loses a 90% cache discount is a loss. OpenAI's flex
  keeps prompt-caching discounts, and Bedrock's Flex reportedly supports explicit caching ([re:Post](https://repost.aws/questions/QUBuhKumN7Qsm6Whi0HaeJuw/flex-service-tier-for-bedrock-models)), but
  provider hopping almost never does. Any "arbitrage" has to price this in.
</Callout>

## The proposal: a deadline-aware router

Most of the "arbitrage" here is really **scheduling**: matching each piece of work to the cheapest tier that still meets its deadline.

<Steps>
<Step>
**Declare deadlines, not tiers.** A goal or step says *when* it needs an answer ("now", "within 15 minutes", "by 7am"). The loop author shouldn't need to know which provider calls that flex, batch or off-peak.
</Step>
<Step>
**Route to the cheapest tier that fits.** Standard for "now", flex for minutes, batch or off-peak windows for hours.
</Step>
<Step>
**Escalate as slack runs out.** On flex rejection, back off and retry; when the remaining slack drops below a threshold, promote to standard (or priority) so the deadline still holds.
</Step>
<Step>
**Keep cache affinity.** Stick with the provider and tier that holds the conversation's cache unless the saving beats the cache loss.
</Step>
<Step>
**Report realized savings per goal.** "This goal cost $8.40; at standard rates it would have been $15.10." That number is what makes anyone trust the router, and it feeds [loop-native reporting](/guide/remedies/loop-reporting).
</Step>
</Steps>

## Two meanings of "idle capacity"

"Use capacity when utilization is low" can mean two quite different things:

1. **The provider's idle capacity.** That's what flex, batch and off-peak pricing *are*: providers selling their troughs. Routing into them is straightforward and within everyone's terms.
2. **Your own idle capacity.** Subscription plans and daily allowances that reset whether you use them or not. Using what's left of **your own** weekly quota for deferrable loop work before it resets is a legitimate, useful scheduling trick. **Reselling** it is something else: a community Hermes plugin, for example, lists a user's unused Venice daily allowance for sale on Surplus Intelligence ([plugin docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/plugins/surplus-intelligence-seller)). Whether that's allowed depends entirely on the provider's terms. Most subscription plans are licensed for the subscriber's own use, so we'd treat resale as out of scope.

## Partner, pass through or build?

| Option | What it means | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Partner** | Recommend or integrate an aggregator (OpenRouter with `:floor`) and add deadline logic on top | Fast; no capital; broad model coverage | Thin margin; the aggregator can ship deadline routing itself |
| **Pass through** | Route over each provider's own batch and flex APIs, using the customer's own keys | No resale; transparent pricing; keeps provider caches and data terms intact | Every provider's tier semantics differ and keep changing |
| **Build a market** | Run an order book for spare capacity, or rent spot GPUs and serve open-weight models | Real spread to capture | Capital, compliance, settlement, model provenance and data-handling questions. Not low-hanging. |

Our current lean: **pass through, with the customer's keys, plus the deadline scheduler.** It's the version that needs no capital, takes no position on pricing, and is useful on day one. The market-building version is interesting, but it's a different company.

## Open questions

- How much loop work is actually deferrable? We'd want [field notes](/guide/field-notes/how-to-contribute) with real splits before believing "most of it".
- Do flex tiers stay cheap once loops pile into them? A tier priced for occasional sheddable traffic may change when it becomes the default for agents.
- Is time-of-day pricing a trend or a one-provider experiment?
