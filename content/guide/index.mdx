This guide is for people who already know what an AI agent is. You've used a coding agent, you know it calls tools and you know roughly how a model decides what to do next. You may still not have a clear picture of what everyone has meant by **"loops"** since mid-2026.

It is not reference documentation. You won't find command flags here (each tool's own docs are linked instead). It explains **concepts** at length: what a loop is, the layers it comes in, how goals relate to it, how different environments implement it, and where it breaks. Every claim links to the source it came from.

## Why loops matter now

Over roughly a year, an idea went from a joke to a product category:

- **July 2025.** Geoffrey Huntley's "Ralph Wiggum" technique is a bash one-liner that feeds the same prompt to a coding agent forever ([Steve Kinney's write-up](https://stevekinney.com/writing/the-ralph-loop)).
- **Spring 2026.** OpenAI ships `/goal` in Codex CLI ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/codex-goals/) calls it "a built-in Ralph loop"). Claude Code, Hermes Agent and Google's Antigravity CLI follow with their own goal modes.
- **June 2026.** Claude Code's creator Boris Cherny says he doesn't prompt Claude any more; he writes loops that do ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/loop-engineering/)). Peter Steinberger and Addy Osmani name the practice **loop engineering** ([O'Reilly Radar](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/loop-engineering/)), and Andrew Ng describes product development as [three nested loops](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-359).
- **July 2026.** The backlash: "it's just a while loop with an LLM call", plus a rebrand attempt as "[graph engineering](https://towardsdatascience.com/graph-engineering-for-ai-agents-from-prompts-and-loops-to-workflows/)".
- **Now.** Loops are standard harness features, and the hard questions have moved elsewhere: *how do you know the loop is done, what does it cost, and who reviews what it produced?*

<Callout title="The one-sentence version">
  An agentic loop is a program that keeps an agent working toward an outcome by deciding, after every
  step, whether to continue. Almost everything interesting about loops is in how that decision gets made.
</Callout>

## A note on evidence

The 2026 discourse about loops is loud and young. Much of it consists of practitioner blogs, vendor content and posts on X. Where a claim rests on thin ground, you'll see a badge:

- <Evidence level="primary" /> the vendor's own docs or engineering blog, or a paper
- <Evidence level="secondary" /> reported by others, not checked against the original
- <Evidence level="anecdotal" /> one person's numbers or story
- <Evidence level="contested" /> sources disagree

As the [August 2026 study of loop engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21884) puts it, the claims attached to the practice "rest almost entirely on anecdotes and self-reported productivity numbers." This guide tries to be honest about that. [How this guide was made](/guide/about) explains the method.
