## What people mean by it

In 2026 the phrase **software factory** came to mean a system where AI agents take work from intake to a mergeable (sometimes deployed) change through a **repeatable pipeline**, and people design the pipeline rather than do the work inside it.

Two definitions show the range:

- **Port**, which runs a research index of company factories: "an AI-powered system where agents, guided by guardrails, shared context, and quality gates, take work from intake to production at scale". It stresses that this is "not a single product but an architecture" ([Port](https://software-factories.port.io/what-is-a-software-factory)). <Evidence level="primary" />
- **PostHog's Jina Yoon**: "Since it's just a metaphor, there's no actual criteria for what counts as a software factory". It's a spectrum of **how much code agents write and how closely humans still read it** ([PostHog](https://newsletter.posthog.com/p/software-factories)). <Evidence level="primary" />

The second definition is the more useful one. Almost every company on the following pages sits somewhere in the middle of that spectrum.

## An old word, a new meaning

The term isn't new. Bob Bemer used it in 1968, Japanese firms such as Hitachi and Toshiba ran "software factories" in the 1970s and 80s, Microsoft published a *Software Factories* methodology in 2004, and the US Department of Defense uses it for its DevSecOps platform teams ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_factory)). All of these meant **standardised process and reusable parts operated by people**.

The 2026 meaning swaps the workers. Three things pushed it into circulation:

- **January 2026.** Dan Shapiro's "[The Five Levels: from Spicy Autocomplete to the Dark Factory](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/)" and Simon Willison's write-up of [StrongDM's AI team](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/), which runs a factory where nobody reads the code. Steve Yegge ships [Gas Town](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/welcome-to-gas-town-4f25ee16dd04), a multi-agent orchestrator.
- **February to April 2026.** OpenAI's "[Harness engineering](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/)" post (an internal product of roughly a million lines, none written by hand) and then [Symphony](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4164173/openais-symphony-spec-pushes-coding-agents-from-prompts-to-orchestration.html), a spec for turning an issue tracker into a control plane for agents.
- **June to November 2026.** Vendors adopt the word: [Factory 2.0](https://factory.ai/news/software-factory), Warp Factories, 8090's Software Factory, Islo's Enterprise Software Factory. Port holds a "[first AI Software Factory Summit](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/port-announces-portcon-first-ai-153800616.html)" in November.

## Shapiro's five levels

Shapiro's ladder, modelled on driving automation, became the common way to say how far along a team is. As summarised by [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jan/28/the-five-levels/):

| Level | Name | What the human does |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Spicy autocomplete | Writes the code; accepts suggestions |
| 1 | The coding intern | Delegates boilerplate; reviews all of it |
| 2 | The junior developer | Pair programs; still reviews every line |
| 3 | The developer | Most code is AI-written; the human is a full-time reviewer |
| 4 | The engineering team | Acts as manager or PM; writes specs and plans with the agents |
| 5 | The dark software factory | "A black box that turns specs into software." Nobody reviews the code |

The name "dark factory" borrows from manufacturing plants that run with the lights off because robots don't need to see. **Level 5 is rare.** Most of what's called a software factory in 2026 is level 3 or 4 with automation around it: agents write the code, humans still approve every merge.

## How a factory relates to loops

A factory isn't a new kind of loop. It's what you get when the [four layers](/guide/foundations/four-layers) are wired together and **left running**:

| Factory piece | Loop concept | Where the guide covers it |
|---|---|---|
| The factory's mission ("keep open issues near zero") | A [standing goal](/guide/goals/standing-goals) | Goals |
| Webhooks, labels, Slack mentions, schedules | [Ambient and scheduled loops](/guide/patterns/ambient-loops) (Layer 3) | Patterns |
| One work item, built until checks pass | A goal loop (Layer 2) | [Foundations](/guide/foundations/four-layers#layer-2--the-goal-loop) |
| Separate review, test and verification stations | [Maker/checker](/guide/patterns/maker-checker) | Patterns |
| The board or tracker routing work between stations | [Graphs and teams](/guide/patterns/graphs-and-teams) (Layer 4) | Patterns |

The practical difference from a goal loop is **who starts it and who watches it**. A goal loop is something a person kicks off and checks on. A factory is started by events, processes many items at once, and is judged by throughput and quality over weeks, not by whether one run finished.
