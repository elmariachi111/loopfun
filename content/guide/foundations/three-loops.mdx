In his June 2026 letter in *The Batch* ([issue 359](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-359)), Andrew Ng described building new products with AI agents as **three nested loops running at different speeds**. It is one of the most useful framings of the year, because it puts the machine loop in its proper place: inside human loops.

Ng's example was a typing-practice app for his daughter. Per [secondary summaries](https://explainx.ai/blog/andrew-ng-three-loops-0-to-1-products-2026), the agent ran for about an hour unattended, using a browser to check the UI itself. <Evidence level="secondary" />

## Why this matters for loop design

- **Faster inner loops make the outer loops the bottleneck.** If the agent can produce a working version in twenty minutes, the limit is how fast a person can look at it and say what's wrong. A [summary of Ng's framing](https://www.besthub.dev/articles/andrew-ng-s-three-layer-loop-faster-agents-demand-slower-human-feedback-c526f15b245f) puts it as "faster agents demand slower human feedback". This connects directly to the [review bottleneck](/guide/shortcomings/humans).
- **Evals are how you move work from the middle loop to the inner one.** Anything you can encode as an automated check, the agent can iterate on without you. Anything you can't stays in the developer loop. This is the same insight as [writing goals that can be checked](/guide/goals/writing-goals).
- **Not everything belongs in the inner loop.** The external loop, real-world feedback, can't be simulated away. Loops shine when there's a reliable signal close at hand and struggle when the true signal is days away.
