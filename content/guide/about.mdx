## What this is

loop.fun is an explanatory guide to agentic loops: what they are, how today's harnesses run them, how goals relate to them, and where they fall short. It's written for people who already understand AI agents, and it tries to explain concepts rather than document commands.

## Method

The first version was compiled in **November 2026** with AI research assistants (two different assistants researched the topic independently, and their findings were merged). The research screened:

- vendor engineering blogs and docs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Nous Research)
- practitioner essays and newsletters
- X posts, Hacker News threads and GitHub issues
- arXiv papers and one EMNLP tutorial

Every claim links to where it came from. The [source library](/guide/sources) collects them all.

## Limits

<Callout type="warn" title="Read with care">
  - **Much of the research worked from search-result extracts**, not complete reading of every article. Where something
    matters, follow the link and read the original.
  - **The 2026 discourse is noisy.** Many sources are SEO content or vendor blogs with an interest in the topic. Dates,
    version numbers and dollar figures often disagree between sources.
  - **Tools change weekly.** The environment pages describe a snapshot and will drift.
</Callout>

The badges are an attempt at honesty about all this:

- <Evidence level="primary" /> the vendor's own docs or engineering writing, or a research paper
- <Evidence level="secondary" /> reported by others, not checked against the original
- <Evidence level="anecdotal" /> one person's numbers or story
- <Evidence level="contested" /> sources disagree

## Help make it better

The guide gets better as people who actually run loops add to it. The most valuable thing you can add is your own story: [share a field report](/guide/field-reports). Every page also has links to improve it or start a discussion.
